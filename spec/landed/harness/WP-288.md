# WP-288 — the benchmark baseline at 1.0.0 and what to do next

A measurement package: no change under `crates/` or `xtask/`. Landed:
the two baselines `bench/baselines/aarch64-implementer-0x61-part-0x000-24c.tsv`
and `...-x86_64.tsv` with their logs (refreshed by `--update-baseline`,
below), `docs/status/perf.md` regenerated from those runs by
`cargo xtask bench --report`, and this record. The code generation
measured is v1.0.0's (`0e887d04`) plus `!exclude` (hod version 1.0.0),
release profile (fat LTO, one code unit, `debug = 1`).

## Method

- Host: the shared development machine (aarch64, 24 cores), with other
  agents building on it. The load average is beside every wall-clock
  table; it was 13 to 26 throughout. x86-64 objects are a cross run on the
  same host (`--target x86_64`), as the x86-64 baseline always was.
- Wall time and peak memory: `cargo xtask bench` as specified (fastest of
  N serial runs, middle peak RSS from GNU `time`, `llc` in the same
  rounds), `--runs 5`, LLVM 22 and LLVM 19, both targets, with `-v`
  (phases) and, for LLVM 22, `--instructions`.
- Anything used to rank: callgrind instruction counts, `HOD_THREADS=1`.
  Attribution is by self cost (`callgrind_annotate --inclusive=no
  --auto=no --threshold=100`), each function counted to the crate named
  in its symbol: with fat LTO and recursion, callgrind's inclusive costs
  are not meaningful (they come out above the program total), so there
  is no inclusive top list. `std`/`core` code inlined into a hod function
  counts to that function's crate; what remains in `std`, `core` or libc
  is shown as its own row.
- Thread scaling: hod's own phase report (`HOD_TIME_PHASES`), 7 runs per
  thread count, fastest per phase.
- Allocations: `valgrind --tool=dhat`, `HOD_THREADS=1`.
- End to end: `kiln++ -std=c++23 -O0 [-g] -c` on the bench's C++
  sources (the pinned kiln release build), `KILN_BACKEND=llvm` against
  `KILN_BACKEND=hod KILN_HOD=<this hod>`, 5 interleaved rounds, fastest
  wall time, kiln's own `KILN_STATS_LOG` for the split (front end, IR
  emission and printing, child tools). aarch64 only.
- Links: the objects of `llc-22 -O0 -relocation-model=pic` and of hod for
  8 bench programs, linked by the `gcc`/`g++` driver's collect2 line
  (`-fno-use-linker-plugin`), run directly as GNU ld 2.44,
  `ld.lld-22 --threads=1` and `mold 2.37.1 --thread-count=1 --no-fork`;
  callgrind instructions of the linker, fastest of 5 wall times, the
  executable's size, and the program run (every one exited 0). aarch64
  only (WP-175's parked note has the x86-64 picture, which agrees).

Not measured: x86-64 end to end and x86-64 links (kiln cross-compiling
needs a target sysroot the host does not have set up for this); cold
starts on a local filesystem (`docs/VALIDATION.md` "Performance": the
binary would have to be copied outside the workspace); libc++ tests and
corpus modules (only the bench inputs); hod under a real jobserver
(threads were granted with `HOD_THREADS`). Wall-clock numbers below are
from a loaded machine: ratios to `llc` from the same rounds are the
figures to read, absolute times are not.

Function-level parallel code generation is there (spec/cli.md
"Threads") and works where bodies dominate: `bodies` scales 2.5x at 4
threads. It stops at the serial phases: with `-g`, the module-level
`parse` (which reads every metadata node) is 60 to 69 ms of the 139 to
170, and with `read`, `object` and `write` the serial part is about 75%
of stl-heavy-g's time at 8 threads. One function (`huge-function`) does
not parallelise by construction, and `tiny-functions` (5,001 functions of
a few instructions) does not scale at all. Without `--threads`,
`HOD_THREADS` or a jobserver hod uses one thread, which is what a kiln
compile outside `make` gets.

## 3. Process overhead

hod on a one-function module (from a file; from standard input as kiln
gives it) and on empty input (`-mtriple`, an empty module), aarch64,
fastest of 30, load 20:

| run | wall | instructions | peak RSS | minor / major faults |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/bin/true` | 1.1 ms | | 0.6 MiB | 75 / 0 |
| `hod --version` | 4.2 ms | 0.37 M | 2.0 MiB | 161 / 7 |
| hod, empty module | 10.4 ms | 0.46 M | 3.3 MiB | 249 / 23 |
| hod, one function, file | 11.1 ms | 0.50 M | 4.1 MiB | 202 / 36 |
| hod, one function, stdin | 13.7 ms | | 4.3 MiB | 259 / 36 |
| `llc-22`, one function | 11.8 ms | | 59.1 MiB | 2307 / 0 |

Of the 0.50 M instructions, the dynamic loader's relocation and symbol
lookup is 0.23 M (47%), libc start-up (`vfscanf`, tunables) most of the
rest; hod's own work is under 0.1 M, the 57 µs of design 0004 §1.4
unchanged. The wall time is the operating system's: the major faults
are hod's 5.2 MB text segment paged in from the bind-mounted workspace
(`docs/VALIDATION.md` "Performance", cold timings); design 0004 §1.4's
"1-5 ms" for a process holds on a local filesystem, and this host's
mount makes it 10 ms. Per kiln job that is 1% to 25% of the back end's
time (8 to 9 ms of `small.cpp`'s 39 ms job; under 10 ms of 145 on
stl_heavy -g).

**For the bridge:** in process, a kiln job would save the process (5 to
10 ms), kiln's printing of the text (40 ms on stl_heavy -g) and hod's
reading of it (the text reader is 56% of hod's instructions; about 80 ms
of the 145), some 125 ms of a 1598 ms compile (8%); on templates -g
about 26 + 50 + 10 of 445 ms (19%). That is more than any change inside
hod can give a kiln compile, and it needs kiln's half.

## 6. The ranked list

Gains are instructions at `HOD_THREADS=1` on the named inputs (x86-64)
unless a wall time is named. "Byte-neutral" means the objects must stay
identical, checked with `cmp` over the bench inputs, `tests/ir` and both
corpora (the rule of every speed package).

1. **Debug metadata reading** (`hod_ll::debug`: `build_debug`,
   `node_body`, `static_field`, `field_name`, `node_ref`,
   `define_tuples`; `hod_ir::debug`: `DebugInfo::node`, `di_field`,
   `DiNode::set`). Evidence: 153 M + 162 M of stl-heavy-g's 1312 M (24%),
   90 + 80 M of templates-g; serial: the `parse` phase is 61 ms of 139
   at 4 threads. Expected: −100 to −150 M on stl-heavy-g (−8% to −11%),
   −50 M on templates-g; the same in wall time of the serial phase.
   Byte-neutral. 2 sessions. No dependency.
2. **The lexer, resuming WP-176** (`hod_ll::lexer`: `next_token`, `word`,
   `compound`, `word_rule`, `number`; `Token` as a scalar pair; the
   replay cut kept in its note). Evidence: lexer 320 M on stl-heavy-g
   (24%), 108 M on stl-heavy, 120 M of huge-function's 375 M; the parked
   cut gave −5 M on stl-heavy, and its next steps aim at the per-token
   frame and copies (~37 instructions in `expect`). Expected: −15% to −20%
   of the lexer: −50 to −65 M on stl-heavy-g (−4% to −5%), −20 M on
   huge-function. Byte-neutral. 2 sessions. No dependency.
3. **The in-process bridge** (kiln's half; hod's is done, design 0004).
   Evidence: section 3: saves the process, kiln's printing and hod's
   text reading: ~125 ms of 1598 (8%) on stl_heavy -g, ~85 of 445 (19%)
   on templates -g, more than all of items 1 and 2 together for a kiln
   compile. Byte-neutral by construction (the same `hod_ir`). hod: 1
   session of support; kiln: their package. Depends on kiln's
   maintainers.
4. **Parallel or overlapped module-level work** (`hod_ll` metadata parse
   split by node ranges, or the object layout and write overlapped with
   code generation). Evidence: serial phases are ~75% of stl-heavy-g at
   8 threads (read 30, parse 69, object 12, write 15 of 170 ms). Expected:
   wall only, under a jobserver or `--threads`: −30 to −40 ms of 140 on
   stl-heavy-g (−20% to −25%), nothing at one thread. Byte-neutral (the
   chunks are merged in order already). 3 sessions. After item 1 (do
   the serial work cheaper first, then split what is left).
5. **Symbols and constants in `hod_ir`** (`SymbolTable::named`,
   `ConstPool::intern`, `Symbol::kind`). Evidence: 22 M of `tables`' 99 M
   (22%; the input where hod is only 0.37x of `llc`), 14 M of
   tiny-functions-g. Expected: −15 M on tables (−15%), −10 M on
   tiny-functions-g. Byte-neutral. 1 session.
6. **Smaller objects: section names into `.strtab`, shared
   `.debug_addr` entries, one line sequence per section** (`hod_obj`,
   `hod_dwarf` `die.rs`, `hod_cg::emit::lines`; WP-175 causes 4 to 6).
   Evidence: stl-heavy-g's object is 5.57 MB against 4.98 MB: `.shstrtab`
   +495 KB, `.debug_addr` +105 KB; tiny-functions-g +210 KB of line
   table; GNU ld +11% on tiny-functions-g. Expected: −10% of object bytes
   on `-g` inputs, the `object` and `write` phases (26 ms of
   stl-heavy-g) by about as much, links 1% to 10%. **Not byte-neutral**:
   needs spec rules in `spec/object.md` and `spec/dwarf.md`, and the
   debugger tests (L6) as its check. 2 sessions.
7. **Resume WP-175: same-section calls resolved (committed on its
   branch, `ec51c3c`), then address reuse and callee-saved registers**
   (`hod_cg` instruction selection, value locations). Evidence: links
   cost 0% to 4% more with lld and mold, so the link-time gain is small;
   the text is 17% to 37% larger (x86-64 huge-function 1.69x), which is
   exe size and page cache, not compile time. Expected: link ≤ 2%; hod
   itself ~0 (`encoding` is 8 M of 1312 M). Not byte-neutral (code
   changes; irdiff L4 and the corpora as checks). 1 session for
   `ec51c3c`'s remaining checks, 3+ for the rest. Rank: below the
   reader work by the numbers.
8. **`-g` DWARF emission** (`hod_dwarf`: `check_expr`, `slot_fragment`,
   `Writer::die`, `reference_type`). Evidence: 63 M of 1312 M (4.8%) on
   stl-heavy-g, 37 M on templates-g. Expected: −20 M (−1.5%).
   Byte-neutral. 1 session. Worth doing after 1 and 2, not before.
9. **`tiny-functions` does not scale with threads** (the chunking of
   `hod::back` for many small functions; `PARALLEL_MIN_INSTS`).
   Evidence: total 64.7 ms at 1 thread, 85.7 ms at 8; `bodies` 12.8 to
   14.0. Expected: wall only; at best `bodies` and `emission` halved at
   4 threads (−10 ms of 65). Byte-neutral. 1 session (measure first:
   the cause is not known).
10. **Process start** (link hod with fewer dynamic relocations, or
    statically). Evidence: 0.23 M of a trivial compile's 0.50 M
    instructions is the dynamic loader; the wall is page faults of the
    binary. Expected: −0.2 M instructions, ~0.1 ms; the faults are the
    filesystem's. Byte-neutral. Not worth a package now: the bridge
    removes the process altogether.
11. **Allocation policy v2: not worth doing.** 109,600 allocations on
    stl-heavy-g, `malloc` and `free` under 1% of the instructions.
12. **Peak-memory reductions: not worth doing now.** hod's peak is 0.07x
    to 0.23x of `llc`'s on every input (36 MiB on stl-heavy-g against
    152); in a kiln compile the peak is kiln's (119 MiB of the job
    through hod). Of hod's 34 MB heap peak, 16 MB is the input itself,
    which the bridge removes.

## 7. The page and the gate

The baselines were refreshed by the procedure of spec/harness.md
"Baselines": `cargo xtask bench --update-baseline --reason "<text>"`
with all inputs, clang 22, `--runs 15` (the spec's alternative to a
quiet machine), once per target. The aarch64 baseline it replaces was
recorded when hod declined every input (2026-11-12), so it judged
nothing; the x86-64 one was WP-153's (2026-11-13, hod built 17 of the
20 inputs). The
load during the refresh was 26 to 35 on 24 cores (it rose while the
runs were made). The memory half of the gate binds (peak RSS repeats to
a few hundred KiB at any load); the time half does not: for both targets the
update reported all 20 inputs with a time spread above 5%, so the gate
will not fail on any of their times (`llc`'s spread: median 18.9% and
13.1%, largest 43.5% and 46.9%). The sums of the fastest runs at that
load were 0.21x (aarch64) and 0.20x (x86-64) of `llc`'s, against 0.18x
and 0.16x at load 14 to 21 an hour earlier: the ratio moves with the
load too. The instruction counts repeated (hod 5594.0 M and 5712.4 M
against 5594.4 M and 5714.3 M).
On a quiet machine the time baselines should be recorded again with the
same commands:

```
cargo xtask bench --update-baseline --reason "<why>" --runs 15 --instructions
cargo xtask bench --update-baseline --reason "<why>" --runs 15 --instructions --target x86_64
cargo xtask bench --report
```

`docs/status/perf.md` is `cargo xtask bench --report` over those two
runs.

**Can the gate be turned on in `cargo xtask ci` today?** Not as
specified. `--check` against a binding baseline on this host returns
NOISY (exit status 3) for most time verdicts, by spec/harness.md "Where
the gate means something", and the gate's steps share the machine by
design; it also adds 2 to 3 minutes of serial measurement. What stops it
is the time half: a gate step that judged memory only (and large time
regressions past the drift limit) would hold here, but the command has no
such mode, and adding one is a harness package of its own.
