# WP-130 — x86-64 groups together (design 0006)

Landed: `crates/hod_x64/tests/diff.rs`; six `tests/ir/drift_*.ll` seeds
(`spec/harness.md`, the seed table and the risk-7 paragraph);
`xtask corpus reasons --target` (`spec/harness.md` "Other
architectures"); `docs/status/reasons-x64.md`;
`crates/hod_testutil/gen/{int_gen,control_gen}.rs`.

Tests only: no code generator changed.

## Whole programs

`crates/hod_x64/tests/diff.rs` takes random whole programs from
`hod_interp::generate` with x86-64's set of groups (the defaults plus
`block_addresses`; vectors and exceptions off until WP-132 and the
x86-64 unwinder). It compiles each batch of programs ONCE per leg — hod
plainly, hod with every stress option, `llc -O0 -fast-isel=0` of LLVM
22 and 19 — links them with one C driver and runs ONE emulated process
per leg, and compares every program's result against the interpreter's.

- `programs_agree_with_the_interpreter`: 600 programs (2 batches).
- `programs_without_one_group_agree`: 150 each without memory, calls,
  floats.
- `a_mutated_program_is_caught`: a wrong result and an extra call put
  into one program are reported by both hod legs, not by llc's.
- `call_widths_seed_stressed`: `drift_call_widths.ll` built by
  `hod_x64` directly prints what the seed says, plainly and stressed.
- `many_programs_agree` (ignored; `HOD_DIFF_SEEDS`): 20,000 programs
  agreed on all four legs, 0 skipped, in 343.6 s (17.2 s per 1000).

## Seeds

One shared drift seed per risk-7 rule no seed covered: `drift_fp128_libm`,
`drift_call_widths`, `drift_saturation`, `drift_dead`, `drift_bfloat`,
`drift_lanes`. Each passes on AArch64 and as the `llc` reference on
x86-64, with every linker and LLVM 22 and 19; on this package's branch
hod built five of them for x86-64, and `drift_lanes` since WP-132
landed with it.

The classify rows `attr:zeroext`, `attr:signext` and `attr:byref` were
stale `todo(X64)` (WP-125 lowers them): they are ON for x86-64, the same
edit as the gate tree's.

Strict: every `tests/ir` seed hod builds for x86-64 builds with
`--strict` too — 46 of 75 objects with LLVM 22 and 19, 0 FAIL (41 of 65
before). The rest are refused by name: `inst:extractvalue/double`
(aggregate), `unsupported-target` (`const_operands` pins an AArch64
triple), coroutine seeds (WP-137), `type:vector`
(WP-132), `inst:fptoui/int-wide` and `va_copy` (WP-144), `x86_fp80`
(WP-131).

## Corpus

The sample (`.build/x64-sample-138`, 375 modules): hod built 220, refused
155 by name, 0 crashes.

`corpus verify --target x86_64 -j 4` (hybrid), all 6,244 entries, after
the classify rows above:

| LLVM | tests | built by hod | results | PASS | FAIL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 22 | 3,140 | 2,941 | 9,420 | 9,417 | 3 |
| 19 | 3,104 | 2,907 | 9,312 | 9,309 | 3 |

(Before them: 2,581 and 2,553 built, the same FAILs.) The FAILs are one
entry, gcc-torture `builtins/strncmp.c`, on three linkers: NOT a wrong
result. Its library `strncmp` returns `c1 - c2` of two uninitialized
locals when `n` is 0 (undefined in C); `llc`'s frame happens to hold
equal bytes there, hod's does not. Initialising the two locals makes
hod's build pass.

Refusals by name and their owners: `docs/status/reasons-x64.md`. The top
first refusals now: `type:vector` (67 LLVM 22 modules, WP-132),
`type:x86_fp80` (38, WP-131), `operand:int-of-addr` (27, no owner),
`intrinsic:llvm.eh.sjlj.longjmp`/`setjmp` (18 and 14, no owner),
`inst:extractvalue/double` (17, WP-144).

## Text size

`.text` of every single-module LLVM 22 entry hod builds strictly (2,799),
hod against `llc -O0` with the entry's arguments, from `llvm-objdump -d`:

| percentile | hod bytes | llc bytes | hod / llc |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 151 | 74 | 1.70 |
| 50 | 398 | 201 | 2.01 |
| 90 | 1,743 | 897 | 2.38 |
| 99 | 16,411 | 8,562 | 3.27 |
| all | 10,044,133 | 5,880,808 | 1.71 |

Where hod's 4.16 MB more go: moves to and from `rbp` slots 1.53 MB
(2.84 MB against 1.31 MB, 37%); unrolled copies through `xmm15` 0.63 MB
(15%, 0.47 MB of it in three gcc-torture modules with large `byval`
arguments or copies); the five saved registers 0.44 MB (246,430 pushes
and pops against 270, 11%); `rel32` branches that reach with `rel8`
0.31 MB (7.5%; hod has fewer branches, 146,016 against 234,182, but
4.9 bytes each against 2.4).

Proposals (not implemented):

1. `crates/hod_x64/src/frame.rs`, the prologue and epilogue: push and
   pop only the members of `SAVED` the body's allocation used (known
   after the body; the prologue emitted or patched then, its CFI rows
   with it). Up to 0.44 MB, 4.4% of hod's `.text`; 18 bytes in a
   function that uses none.
2. `crates/hod_x64/src/common.rs`, `branch` and `branch_cond`: a forward
   branch to the block laid out next is omitted, and the others shrink
   to `rel8` when bound in reach (a relaxation pass over the function's
   fixups before it is copied out). Up to 0.31 MB, 3.1%.
3. `crates/hod_x64/src/common.rs`, `copy_frame` and `copy_from_ptr`:
   above 64 bytes (`INLINE_MAX`), a counted loop as `intrinsic.rs`
   `copy_loop` does. Up to 0.6 MB, 6%, most of it in three modules
   (pr28982b: 278 KB of copies).

The slot moves, the largest share, are hod's design (every value lives
in its slot between instructions); no proposal here.

## Generators

`int_gen` (tables and the random straight-line generator; the edge
values are the union of both targets') and the control-flow graph
generator moved to `crates/hod_testutil/gen/`, included by `#[path]`
from both crates' `tests/common` files: no new dependency, every
existing test of both crates passes. What stays per target: the compile,
link and run, and `result_mask` (AArch64 compares an `i1` at its low
byte, x86-64 at bit 0). `mem_gen` and `aggr_gen` stay: x86-64's add an
FP register class and restructure the aggregate generator.
