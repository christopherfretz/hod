# WP-063 — bodies parsed and read in parallel

Landed: layered tables, a seed and forks in `hod_ir` (`spec/ir.md` §5.1,
"Reading bodies in parallel: a seed and forks"), the generic driver
`hod_ir::read_bodies` (`crates/hod_ir/src/parallel.rs`),
`hod_ll::parse_bodies_on` (`spec/ll.md` §11.1),
`hod_bc::read_bodies_on` (`spec/bc.md` §7, §11.1), and the facade's use of
them (`crates/hod/src/front.rs`). Design 0002 was the plan; its status line
says where this differs from it.

## What it is

- The layer is a type parameter (`Layering`, sealed) of every table a
  body adds to (types, constants, inline assembly, function attribute
  sets, debug expressions and locations) and of `Module`. `NoLayer`,
  zero-sized and the default, is the module and the one-thread reader:
  a `Seg<T, NoLayer>` is a `Vec`, an `InternTable<NoLayer>` has no base,
  the tables a body only reads (`Shared`) are owned in place, and the
  only addition is an edit counter for `Module::stamp`. `Layered` (a
  shared `Arc` base, a local layer, global indices; a hash table that
  probes its base first) is for forks only. The public names are the
  `NoLayer` types (`Module` is `Module<NoLayer>`), so producers that feed
  a `Session` are unchanged.
- `hod_ll`'s `Parser`/`Fx` and `hod_bc`'s `Fx`/`Dx` are generic over the
  layer and instantiated twice; the `Layered` instantiation runs only
  when bodies are read on more than one thread.
- `Module::seed` copies the tables once per parallel read into a
  `Module<Layered>`; `seed.fork()` shares all of it and copies a few
  words. `mark`/`delta` take what one body added, `adopt` interns it into
  the module in the fork's order and extends a `Remap`, `Body::remap`
  translates the body (its lifetime markers too, WP-104). An empty part
  of a delta is `None`: no table is built for it. The address-taken list
  of `blockaddress` constants (WP-064) is adopted through `block_addr`.
- The driver hands runs of consecutive bodies of about 16 KiB of text
  (`CHUNK_WEIGHT`, `BodySource::weight`; bitcode counts its bytes four
  times) to scoped threads, at most four runs per thread ahead of the
  last delivered. The calling thread adopts and delivers body by body in
  file order, and reads runs itself while the next is not ready.
- A body that fails in a fork, or changes what a fork cannot hand over,
  is read again on the module in its turn: its error is the sequential
  reader's, and the first in file order is returned. If that read
  changed what the forks share (`Module::stamp`), the rest is read
  sequentially.
- The facade reads on `read_threads(body bytes, bodies, granted)`
  threads (`crates/hod/src/front.rs`, a pure function, tested in
  `crates/hod/tests/read_threads.rs`): one if fewer than two bodies or
  fewer than 512 bytes of body text per body on average, else at most
  4, at most what is granted, and one per 128 KiB of body text (bitcode
  counts half its bits as bytes). Reading and code generation make one
  request to the token pool per compile (`HeldTokens`): reading takes
  and holds the tokens, code generation takes its share of them and the
  rest go back at once, and all are given back at the end, also on a
  refusal. With `--threads`/`HOD_THREADS` the threads are granted
  outright. `crates/hod/tests/back.rs:138` still sees one request.

## Determinism

Interning is by content, and deltas are adopted in file order, so every
new entry is made in the order the sequential reader makes it: the
bodies, every table entry and id, the unsupported record and the error
are the sequential reader's at any thread count.

- `crates/hod_ll/tests/parallel.rs`: hand-written modules; randomized
  modules whose bodies race for the same new types and constants, with
  forward references and huge and tiny bodies mixed (seeds 1 to 6);
  clang 22's C++ and C with and without `-g`; a malformed body in the
  middle and another after it (the first is returned, 17 bodies
  delivered); an error late in the last huge body. Each at 1, 2, 3, 8
  and 24 threads, twice, against the sequential reading: every body,
  every table entry by id, the unsupported record, the error, and no
  body read again on a clean input.
- `crates/hod_bc/tests/parallel.rs`: clang 22's C++ as bitcode with and
  without `-g` (more than 40 bodies), the checked-in `.bc` files, and
  bytes flipped in two bodies over 24 shifts.
- Corpus after the type parameter and the merge of main `65ec417`
  (`corpus-bytes.py` with the head's `HOD_THREADS` set, real thresholds):
  at 1, 2 and 4 threads, 6,441 objects byte-identical and 10 declines
  identical to main's at one thread.
- Corpus before it (`scripts/corpus-bytes.py`, 6,451 text modules, `-j 4`), with a
  build whose thresholds were set to 1 byte so that every module of more
  than one body is read in parallel (built, then reverted): at 2 and at 8
  threads, 6,425 objects byte-identical and 26 declines identical to the
  branch point's. With the real thresholds at 8 threads, the same.
- Mutations, each caught and reverted: results merged in arrival order
  rather than file order (4 of 5 `hod_ll` tests fail); the lookup that
  skips the base table (4 of 5); the last error returned rather than the
  first (`the_first_error_in_file_order_is_returned`). Rerun after the
  type parameter, each caught again by `hod_bc/tests/parallel.rs` and
  by `hod_ll/tests/parallel.rs` (`.build` logs `mut-*.log`).

## One thread

hod's default use is one thread per compile, and there reading goes
through `sequential` with the `NoLayer` instantiation: no seed, no fork,
no delta, no layer test.

**Measuring cold.** No `perf` here. `pp.py` runs each binary as a new
process and the "parse" phase is where most of its code is first
touched. On this host the time of those first touches depends on the
binary *file's* page-cache state: a byte-identical copy of the base read
+86% on regex parse against the base itself in one round, with the run
order rotated. Fair rounds therefore copy every binary afresh, at once,
and include such a copy as a control.

**On a local filesystem** (the container's overlay; binaries and inputs
copied there, 16 runs, rotated order, load 14 to 20, median / minimum
µs; "b2" is main `65ec417`, "bb" its byte-identical copy, the control,
"t2" this package; "wall" is process start to exit as the parent sees
it):

There parse is 11 to 25% slower and tiny.ll pays about 2 ms: the same
extra faults, each a file-backed fault through the mount. So the
one-thread rule is met where hod runs; cold timings are taken on a local
filesystem (`docs/VALIDATION.md`, "Performance").

**Cause: text size.** The cost that remains is code size, not work on the path:
- `.text`: 2,206,744 bytes at main, 2,820,280 now (+614 KB), 2,442,472
  with the parallel reader compiled out (+236 KB). Before the type
  parameter (`f6d023f`, on the old base) it was +143 KB.
- Release builds do not share generic instantiations between crates
  (`-Zshare-generics` is unstable), and LTO does not merge identical
  ones. Every crate that calls a table method of `X<NoLayer>` gets its
  own copy, where the base had one in `hod_ir`. In the nopar binary,
  for example, `DebugInfo::node` has 4 copies, `ConstPool::float` and
  the `fold_*` functions 3, `flatten::Writer::write` 5, and `Parser`'s
  `parse_const`/`parse_type`/`node_body` 2 (in `hod_ll` and in `hod`,
  where `parse_bodies_on::<H>` instantiates the reader).
- Bisected on the old base, on the mount, interleaved, 15 runs: the
  commits up to `7f7c230` (the layered tables with an inline test on
  every access) are the base's on parse; `4bb4bb1` (nothing on the
  module-level path) is +11 to +20%. Parse there moves with code
  layout, not with the one-thread logic.

Follow-up for text size (+614 KB, and the 1 MB of resident text on
stl-heavy), untried: (1) `BodySource` with two non-generic methods
(`read_body` on `Module`, `read_body_layered` on `Module<Layered>`), so
the readers are instantiated in `hod_ll`/`hod_bc` only and not in
`hod`; (2) the heavy table and writer methods with one `NoLayer` copy
in `hod_ir` (inherent impls on the concrete type, or trait methods of
`Layering`), the layered copy reached only from the parallel path.

## Thresholds

`sweep.py` (synthetic modules, the facade, `read_threads` bypassed,
bodies phase, speed-up over one thread on minimums, load 20 to 30):

- Real units peak at 4 threads: regex `-O0` 3.32x, stl-heavy 3.32x,
  templates `-g` 3.15x. At 6 and 8 threads each is slower than at 4
  (the calling thread adopts every delta). So `READ_MAX_THREADS` = 4.
- Average body size, about 1 MB of text: 216 bytes 0.90x at 2 threads;
  288 bytes 1.05x; 432 bytes 1.44x at 2, 1.36x at 4; 719 bytes 1.45x at
  2, 1.87x at 4. tiny-functions (132 bytes) 0.66x at 2, 0.92x at 4;
  with `-g` 0.69x to 0.93x. So `READ_MIN_BODY_BYTES` = 512.
- Total text, bodies of about 1.3 KB: 42 KB 1.26x at 2; 85 KB 1.53x at
  2; 170 KB 1.45x at 2, 1.67x at 4; 342 KB 2.17x at 4; 685 KB 2.03x at
  4; 1.37 MB 2.23x at 3. Gains below 256 KiB are small and the noise
  on a loaded host is of their size, so `READ_BYTES_PER_THREAD` = 128
  KiB (two threads from 256 KiB, every run then the full 16 KiB of
  `CHUNK_WEIGHT`).

With these, tiny-functions and small modules stay on one thread
(`the_reading_policy` checks both measured units).

## Where it does not scale, and why

- **Past 4 threads.** The calling thread adopts every delta, translates
  every body and runs the session's work on it, serially (Amdahl). At 8
  threads it is the bottleneck, and each further fork re-interns entries
  another fork already handed over, so the deltas grow. On this loaded
  host 8 threads were slower than 4 on every input.
- **Tiny bodies.** At about 250 bytes and 4 instructions a body, adopting
  and translating it on the calling thread costs about what parsing it
  does. tiny-functions is slower at 2 and 4 threads and even at 8. The
  first version (a lock and a `notify_all` per body, and an empty delta
  that still built two hash tables) made it 13 times slower at 8
  threads.
- **One huge body.** huge-function is one body of 78k instructions:
  nothing to share out.

## Left

- Text size: +614 KB of `.text` (generic copies per crate); options
  (1) and (2) under "One thread".
- The merge could move off the calling thread (adopt in batches per
  run), which is what limits 4 threads and more.
- The bitcode path is covered by the tests, not by the corpus (whose
  modules are text) nor by the timings.
- `tests/differential/bodies.rs` still parses on `Module` clones (design
