# WP-188: system registers in AArch64 inline assembly (part A); `alwaysinline:return-address` investigated (part B, parked)

## Part A: `mrs`/`msr` of system registers (`spec/asm.md` §5.4)

- `crates/hod_asm/src/a64/sysreg.rs`: the table `TABLE` (55 names),
  generated once from `llvm-mc-22`: the names it prints, with no
  feature, for the ID-register space (op0 3, op1 0, CRn 0, CRm 0..7) and
  `cntfrq_el0`, `cntvct_el0`, `cntpct_el0`, `tpidr_el0`, `tpidrro_el0`,
  `fpcr`, `fpsr`, `nzcv`, `daif`, `dczid_el0`, `ctr_el0`, `currentel`,
  `rndr`, `rndrrs`, each with the directions `llvm-mc` accepts; the
  generic form `S<op0>_<op1>_C<n>_C<m>_<op2>` with `llvm-mc`'s ranges
  (op0 0..3, no leading zeros). Names are case-insensitive. The words are
  built in `hod_asm` (`0xd520_0000`/`0xd500_0000` with the field at bit
  5): `hod_mc_a64::SysReg` names five registers and is no longer used by
  the template assembler.
- `Features` gains `rand` (`+rand`): `rndr`/`rndrrs` without it are
  `asm:feature`; `+v8.5a` does not imply it in `llvm-mc-22`, so not here
  either.
- Refused by name, `asm:operand/sysreg`: any other name, `msr` to a
  read-only register (`cntvct_el0`, `midr_el1`, `rndr`, ...), the names
  `llvm-mc-22` gates behind other features (`id_aa64zfr0_el1`,
  `id_aa64smfr0_el1`, `id_isar6_el1`, `id_pfr2_el1`, `tpidr2_el0`,
  `tco`, `dit`, `ssbs`, ...: their generic form is accepted), and `msr`
  of an immediate to a PSTATE field (`daifset`, `spsel`). The refusal
  carries no detail: `hod_asm::Refusal` is one static name and the
  pipeline into `hod::Unsupported::detail` has no slot for it
  (follow-up).

Evidence:
- `hod_asm/tests/sysreg.rs`: the table regenerated from `llvm-mc-22`
  equals `TABLE` (55 rows); 156 bodies (every row in each allowed
  direction, upper and lower case, `xzr`; the generic form at four
  corner points for each op0) equal `llvm-mc-22`'s words; 16 refusals.
- `hod_a64/tests/sysreg_asm.rs`: 12 functions (`cntfrq_el0` by name and
  generic, `cntvct_el0` monotonic, `tpidr_el0` equals
  `__builtin_thread_pointer()`, `midr_el1`, `id_aa64isar0_el1`,
  `id_aa64pfr0_el1`, `ctr_el0`, `dczid_el0`, an `fpcr` write/read round
  trip and restore, `nzcv` and `fpsr` writes) built by hod and by
  `llc -O0 -global-isel=0 -fast-isel=0` 19 and 22, linked with a C
  driver that compares each with its own `mrs`, run under every linker:
  `checked, 0 failures` for all three builds; the 17 system-register
  words of hod's code equal `llc`'s with the general register masked
  (the allocators choose different registers).
- abseil (`unscaledcycleclock.cc`, `crc/cpu_detect.cc`, kiln-built
  modules): `hod --strict` with their saved `llc` arguments compiles
  both; text and bitcode objects are byte-identical; the asm words are
  `d53be009 mrs x9, cntfrq_el0` and `d5380009 mrs x9, midr_el1` where
  `llc-22` writes the same instruction with `x8`.
- Mutation: op2 shifted one bit in `sysreg::field` fails both table
  tests; reverted.

## Part B: `alwaysinline:return-address` (parked: `PARKED.md`)

What `opt -passes=always-inline` does, 19 and 22 alike (checked on small
modules):
- it inlines a direct call when the callee is `alwaysinline`, OR the
  call site carries `alwaysinline`, even into a `noinline` callee; a
  call-site `noinline` keeps the call;
- `llvm.returnaddress`, `llvm.frameaddress` (any depth),
  `llvm.addressofreturnaddress` and `llvm.sponentry` are copied
  unchanged into the caller, so they mean the caller's frame;
- it does not inline variadic callees (with or without `va_start`) nor
  callees with `llvm.localescape`; a `musttail` call in the callee
  becomes a plain call.

Both abseil refusals (`TestWithReturnAddress` in `symbolize_test.cc`,
`CaptureBacktraceNoInline` in `stacktrace_test.cc`) are bodies that are
`noinline optnone` and no call site in either module carries
`alwaysinline`: `opt` inlines neither, and the out-of-line call hod
makes IS `opt`'s result. hod declines because `inlining.rs` cannot see
call-site attributes (the readers drop call-site `alwaysinline` and
`noinline`, `hod_ir::CallSite` has no bit for them), so any
`alwaysinline` set in the module turns the check on for every direct
call. Making it precise needs a call-site bit in `hod_ir` and both
readers, outside this package's ownership.
