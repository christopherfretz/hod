Landed: `crates/hod_cg/src/shrink.rs` and `cx.rs` (cut 1),
`crates/hod_mc_x64/src/inst.rs` (cut 2); `cargo xtask bench --target`
and `--instructions` (`xtask/src/bench.rs`, `bench/record.rs`,
`bench/report.rs`, `bench/check.rs`; `spec/harness.md` "bench", "Cross

`bench/baselines/aarch64-implementer-0x61-part-0x000-24c-x86_64.tsv` and

emitted bytes and no new refusal: every cut was checked with `cmp`
against the objects of main 6e300f5 (below).

## Method

The figure compared before and after is callgrind's instruction count
(`valgrind --tool=callgrind`, `HOD_THREADS=1`, the release profile, which
has `debug = 1`): it repeats from run to run on the shared host, where
wall times move by 20% and more (perf.md, "spread"). Attribution is by
each instruction's own source line, grouped by file and crate, with a
line of `std` or libc counted to the crate of the function it was
inlined into or called from (`callgrind_annotate --inclusive=no`):
with fat LTO and one code unit the inclusive costs of callgrind are
not meaningful (inlined frames, cycles).

Inputs: the 20 bench inputs, produced by clang 22 for each target (as
`cargo xtask bench --target` produces them); ten libc++ tests produced
by kiln for each target (`re.alg.match/awk`, `variant.visit/visit`,
`map.modifiers/insert_or_assign`, `format.functions/format`,
`string.modifiers/string_append/size_char`, `alg.sort/sort`,
`ostream.inserters.arithmetic/int`, `time.clock.system/now`,
`vector.modifiers/insert_iter_iter_iter`,
`unord.map.modifiers/insert_or_assign`); four of the corpus's largest
kiln-built x86-64 modules.

Byte identity: 6,367 inputs (`tests/ir`, the x86-64 corpus, the bench
inputs for x86-64, the ten libc++ tests), each built with
`-mtriple=x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu --strict`; hod built 6,005 of them
(the others are malformed corpus files or declines, the same before and
after), and every object and every exit status is the same as main's.

x86-64 runs 8.7% more instructions than AArch64 over the inputs both
built (14,119.7 M against 12,985.8 M). The difference is the back end:
encoding (+359 M: variable-length instructions laid out byte by byte,
where AArch64 writes one word), the shrink pass (+172 M: AArch64 has no
cuts; on x86-64 every frame access that fits `disp8` is a 3-byte cut,
WP-151), and the allocation and copying around them (libc +194 M,
other +136 M). The reader and `hod_ir` are the same work on both
targets; they are half to two thirds of every module.

## Cuts

### 1. Shrink: offsets mapped from a hint, the edits kept (`hod_cg`)

`shrink` mapped every instruction start, every target and every row of
every offset table through a binary search over the pass's edits,
rebuilt the edits after a last pass that changed nothing, and
allocated them afresh per function. Offsets are mapped in increasing
order: a hint (at most 8 linear steps from the last index, then a
binary search on the remaining side) finds the same index; the edits
live in the function's scratch; the final rebuild is skipped when the
last pass changed nothing. Test: `shrink::tests::a_hint_finds_what_a_search_finds`.

| input (x86-64) | before | after | shrink before | after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| stl-heavy | 565,044,215 | 556,279,510 | 8,636,174 | 5,954,729 |
| templates | 509,761,893 | 503,307,199 | 6,934,885 | 4,047,115 |
| tiny-functions | 237,048,578 | 233,740,698 | 4,002,958 | 2,657,092 |
| huge-function | 415,442,063 | 413,232,259 | 3,833,767 | 1,623,415 |
| libc++ re awk | 682,247,906 | 671,350,650 | | |

1.0% to 1.6% of every module; the rest of the saving beyond the pass's
own lines is the allocator's.

### 2. The encoder lays out a `ModRM` form in a register (`hod_mc_x64`)

`modrm`, `plus_r` and `plain` built their bytes in an `Inst` through
`push`, an indexed store with a bounds check and a length kept in
memory, by calling `head` and `mem_operand`; the `Inst` was then copied
out. They now lay the bytes out in a `u128` (byte `i` in bits `8i` to
`8i + 7`) with `head` and `mem_operand` inlined, and make the `Inst`
once at the end, with the same bytes and fields.

| input (x86-64) | after cut 1 | after | encoding before | after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| stl-heavy | 556,279,510 | 553,606,339 | 16,899,032 | 10,137,939 |
| templates | 503,307,199 | 499,919,756 | 17,374,009 | 10,502,846 |
| tiny-functions | 233,740,698 | 231,797,311 | 8,956,499 | 4,924,474 |
| huge-function | 413,232,259 | 410,818,330 | 15,406,435 | 10,329,955 |
| libc++ re awk | 671,350,650 | 667,490,392 | 21,416,069 | 13,260,927 |

The encoder's own lines fall by 33% to 45%; the totals by 0.5% to
0.8%, because part of the work moves with the inlining into the
encoders' callers in `hod_x64`.

Both cuts: stl-heavy 565.0 M to 553.6 M (-2.0%), templates -1.9%,
tiny-functions -2.2%, huge-function -1.1%, re awk -2.2%.

## Peak memory

`/usr/bin/time` maximum resident set, x86-64, strict:

| input | IR | hod, main | hod, cuts 1 and 2 | `llc -O0 -fast-isel=0` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| libc++ `variant.visit` | 59.9 MB | 210,772 KiB, 0.93 s | 212,236 KiB, 0.90 s | 392,308 KiB, 7.10 s |
| libc++ `format` | 36.0 MB | 93,176 KiB, 0.47 s | 94,016 KiB, 0.51 s | 182,912 KiB, 4.54 s |
| libc++ `re awk` | 6.8 MB | 25,340 KiB | 24,508 KiB | |
| stl-heavy | 5.9 MB | 23,492 KiB | 22,928 KiB | |

The cuts do not move the peak (within 1.5 MB, the probe's run-to-run
variation). The heap at its peak on `format` (massif, 98.9 MB) is not
in the x86-64 back end:

- 36.0 MB (36%): the input file, read whole and held through code
  generation (`crates/hod_cli/src/driver.rs:505`, `read_input`).
- about 17 MB: function bodies cloned while they are read
  (`hod_ll::body::parse`, `crates/hod_ll/src/body.rs:274`, through
  `hod_ir::Body::clone`, `crates/hod_ir/src/body.rs:469-493`).
- 8 MiB: the records' code buffer (4.6 MB of text in a doubled
  vector), 8 MiB the records' relocations, and 7.6 MB the object
  model's copy of those relocations (`hod_obj::model::Object::add_relocation`),
  alive together because `crates/hod/src/back.rs:441` hands the chunks
  to the module emitter by reference and drops them after.

## Found elsewhere

- x86-64 declines the three larger `-g` bench inputs (`containers-g`,
  `exceptions-g`, `stl-heavy-g`) with `debug:declare-address`
  (`crates/hod_cg/src/driver.rs:718`, `finish_vars`: the address of a
  `#dbg_declare` is not a frame home), where AArch64 builds all 20.
  The declared addresses are not parameters; which value it is was not
  found. Debug-info territory (WP-149, WP-150).

## Next cuts

- The reader: `hod_ll`'s lexer alone is 22% of stl-heavy on x86-64, the
  reader 30% (tiny-functions) to 66% of every input on both targets, half
  of the sum; `hod_ir` another 5% to 40% (tables, its initialisers).
  Any cut there is worth more than all of the back end's.
- Target features: `X64::for_function` and `hod::back::compile_one`
  parse the `target-features` string of every function (0.5% of all
  instructions on both targets, 2.2% of tiny-functions); a memo per
  attribute group would remove nearly all of it.
- Peak memory: release the input bytes after reading (36% of the heap
  peak on `format`), and move each chunk into the module emitter instead
  of copying its relocations (about 8 MB there).
- The encoder's remaining cost (1.8% of stl-heavy) is the `Inst`
  pushes of immediates and displacements after `modrm`, and the
  `extend_from_slice` per instruction; the shrink pass is down to 1.1%.
