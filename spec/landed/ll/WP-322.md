# WP-322: the copy of every parsed body (measured; no change)

Base `295340dd`. Outcome: the copy `BodyParser::parse` makes of each
body (`crates/hod_ll/src/body.rs:260`, WP-096) stays. Its real cost is
about 6 M instructions on `stl-heavy` (1.4%), not 60 M; every consumer
that `parse` serves keeps the body; and each copy-free shape measured
costs more than the copy. No source file changed.

## Method

Callgrind instruction counts, release profile, `HOD_THREADS=1`,
`hod --strict -O0 -filetype=obj -relocation-model=pic
[-mtriple=x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu] -o out.o in.ll` on the bench
inputs (`cargo xtask bench --generate`, and `--target x86_64`), on the
AArch64 build host. Each variant is a release build of the base with
one change in `BodyParser::parse`, counted whole-program.

## The 60 M figure is an attribution artifact

WP-321 item 3 read 60.1 M inclusive (13.1%) under
`<Body as Clone>::clone`. On this host callgrind's call graph does not
hold there: the same profile shows `clone` "calling"
`hod::front::read::feed` 5,001 times on `tiny-functions` (23.3 M
inclusive, the whole of the session's judging of each body), and
`malloc` with an inclusive cost of 1936% of the program. `clone`'s own
cost is 0.3 M; every `malloc`, `free` and `memcpy` of the whole
compile together is about 25 M. The inclusive figures of a frame on
this host are not a measure; a variant's whole-program count is.

The real cost was measured by copying twice (a second
`clone`, dropped at once, through `black_box`): the difference is one
copy and its free.

## Who keeps a body

| Consumer | Path | Keeps the body past the callback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hod::Session::body` (`crates/hod/src/session.rs:254`), fed by `front/read.rs` `feed` for text and bitcode | `parse_bodies_on` / `read_bodies_on`, sequential and threaded | Yes: every accepted body is pushed to `Session::bodies` and kept until `finish` generates the object (`back.rs` takes `&[Body]`; the bodies go with the object and are freed after it). Only a refused body is dropped at once. `available_externally` bodies go to the inliner, which keeps them too. |
| Threaded reading (`hod_ir::parallel::merge`) | worker threads parse, the calling thread remaps and delivers | Yes, by construction: a body crosses threads in a `Done` queue, so it is owned. |
| `hod_ll::parse_bodies` (`hod_x64/src/target.rs:708`, examples, tests) | lends `&Body` from the parser's storage | No, and no copy is made there already. |
| `hod_bc::read_bodies` | lends `&Body`, recycles the storage | No, and no copy. `hod_bc::BodyReader::read` hands over its storage itself (no copy, no spare-capacity trim) on every path. |

So the premise that the common consumer drops each body before the next
is read does not hold: the one consumer of `parse` on the product path
(the session) keeps all of them. Lending the storage would only move
the copy into `Session::body`, and would change its signature (the
embedding API).

## Measurements (M instructions, whole program; delta against base)

| Input | Target | Base | One copy + free | Hand over always (V1) | Hand over, shrink, new storage at the old capacities (V5) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| stl-heavy | aarch64 | 438.6 | 6.1 | +7.6 | +23.2 |
| stl-heavy | x86-64 | 458.6 | 6.1 | +7.2 | +23.2 |
| stl-heavy-g | aarch64 | 1029.8 | 7.5 | +10.3 | +27.1 |
| stl-heavy-g | x86-64 | 1051.0 | 7.5 | +9.8 | +27.4 |
| templates | aarch64 | 397.9 | 5.4 | +3.8 | +22.8 |
| templates | x86-64 | 422.8 | 5.4 | +3.8 | +22.8 |
| tiny-functions | aarch64 | 172.2 | 6.6 | -0.6 | +7.8 |
| tiny-functions | x86-64 | 180.9 | 6.6 | -0.5 | +7.9 |
| huge-function | aarch64 | 359.4 | 0.0 | -0.0 | +0.0 |
| huge-function | x86-64 | 350.6 | 0.0 | -0.0 | +0.0 |
| small-cxx | aarch64 | 2.9 | 0.03 | +0.01 | +0.1 |
| small-cxx | x86-64 | 3.0 | 0.04 | +0.01 | +0.1 |

- V1 (`parse` returns the storage itself, the next body is built in new
  storage that grows from empty, WP-096's state before its cut 1):
  removes the copy and pays more in growth (one allocation and one
  copying `realloc` per doubling per table), and leaves spare capacity in
  every kept body. Better only on `tiny-functions`, by 0.5 M (0.3%).
- V5 (the storage handed over and shrunk to size with `shrink_to_fit`,
  the next body built in new tables reserved at the old capacities):
  exact-size kept bodies without a copy, but a shrinking `realloc` per
  table costs more than the copy it replaces: +23 M.
- `huge-function` takes the hand-over path already (`HAND_OVER_BYTES`).

Copy per body, from the "one copy + free" column: about 1.8 k
instructions on `stl-heavy` (3,308 bodies), 1.3 k on `tiny-functions`
(5,001 bodies): mostly the allocator (one `malloc` per non-empty table),
little `memcpy`. A shape that does not allocate per table per body would
be the only cut, and that is a change of `Body`'s representation (all
tables in one allocation), not of who owns it.

Peak memory was not measured: no variant was kept.

## What remains (non-`-g` path)

On `stl-heavy` (x86-64) the exclusive profile is flat: the lexer
(`hod_ll::lexer::Lexer::next_token` and `word`, together about 48 M,
10%; `crates/hod_ll/src/lexer.rs`), `memcpy` 8.9 M (1.9%), the
allocator about 15 M (3.3%), `hod_ll::types::parse_type` 6.0 M,
`hod_mc_x64::inst::modrm` 5.7 M, `hod_ll::skip::skip_group` 10.1 M.
Inclusive figures on this host are not to be trusted (above); a cut is
to be judged by a variant's whole-program count.
