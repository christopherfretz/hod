# WP-321 — section names hashed once, member function attributes kept (`hod_cg`, `hod_dwarf`)

A performance package of three ranked items; byte-neutral on every
target. Base cb77ebbc.

## Method

As WP-320: Callgrind instruction counts, `HOD_THREADS=1`, the release
profile, `hod --strict -O0 -filetype=obj -relocation-model=pic
[-mtriple=x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu] -o out.o in.ll` on the bench inputs
(`cargo xtask bench --generate`, and `--target x86_64`).

## Item 1: `Sections::by_name`

`hod_cg::emit::section::Sections::by_name` was a `HashMap<Box<[u8]>,
ByName>` with the standard (SipHash) hasher, and a created section
hashed its name five times (`entry`: `contains_key`, `insert`,
`get_mut`; `record` called `entry` again). The map is only ever read
by `get` and `entry` (no iteration, no `len`, no `keys`; `Debug` is
derived but never printed), so its order cannot reach the object.

- The map is an `IdMap` (`crate::idhash::IdHasher`: FxHash-style words
  with a final mix; its `write(&[u8])` already took 8 bytes at a time
  and the tail). `idhash.rs`'s module text says why section names, which
  come from the input, may use it: a crafted input could cost time,
  never change the object.
- `entry` is one `HashMap::entry` (one hash); `created` pushes the
  section and records the entry size into the same entry
  (`ByName::record`, formerly `Sections::record`). The key is allocated
  even when the name exists; `created` follows a lookup that found no
  section of that name, group and id, which is mostly a new name.
- `explicit` looks the name up once for both the unique-id decision and
  the existing section (the unique id counter is borrowed apart from the
  map).
- Unit test `idhash::tests::section_names_spread`: 10,000
  `.text.<mangled>` names with a long common prefix give at least 9,990
  distinct hashes, at least 9,000 distinct low 16 bits, and spread low
  bytes and top-7-bit tags.

## Item 2: member function attributes computed once

`method_attrs` (all fields but `cont`) runs when a member function DIE
is pushed (`types.rs`, and `spec.rs` for declarations reached through a
definition) and is kept in `UnitPlan::methods`, in DIE order.
`finish_types` walks that list (no node decode, no search) and the
writer finds the entry by binary search; both add `cont` from the
containing types (`kept_method_attrs`). A first version that searched
in `finish_types` too saved nothing: the two searches cost as much as
the decode and the second `method_attrs` they replaced.

## Item 3: the next hot spots of `stl-heavy` (not built)

Callgrind inclusive costs, x86-64, head:

1. `hod_ll/src/body.rs:260` (`Parser::parse`, the copy of each body out
   of the parser's storage, reached from `hod_ir::parallel::deliver_read`,
   `crates/hod_ir/src/parallel.rs:351`): `<Body as Clone>::clone` 60.1 M
   (13.1%), 3,308 calls. The copy is WP-096's choice (exact-size tables
   for the bodies a consumer keeps); a body handed over without the copy,
   or tables allocated once per body, would be the cut.
2. `hod_cg::emit::ModuleEmitter::finish` (`crates/hod_cg/src/emit/mod.rs`):
   one `RawVec` growth (`finish_grow`, a realloc that copies) of 6.4 M
   (1.4%), one call: a large buffer grown without a reservation. The
   line is not resolved (one inlined call site).
3. The drop of `Sections` (`crates/hod_cg/src/emit/section.rs`, the
   `by_name` table: one boxed key and two vectors per name), 1.5 to 2.0 M
   (0.33 to 0.44%), 3,361 frees; and `Sections::entry`'s rehash, 0.5 M
   (five growths: a capacity hint from the number of functions and
   globals would avoid them). Names in one byte arena, with the table
   holding ranges, would remove both.

Not counted: an inclusive 34 M under `Module::new` attributed to
`hashmap_random_keys` (one call) and the same 34 M under
`hod_ll::parser::run_split`'s `RawVec<u8>::grow_one`: one-call frames
whose inclusive cost is the parse itself, not an allocation.
