# WP-357 — the speed figures of the 1.1.0 candidate

A measurement package: no change under `crates/` or `xtask/`. It repeats
WP-288's two tables (hod against `llc -O0`, and kiln end to end) for the
1.1.0 release candidate, adds a third (hod 1.0.0 against the candidate on
the same inputs), and regenerates `docs/status/perf.md` from the first
table's run. Nothing here gates the tag (`docs/status/perf.md` is not a
condition of `docs/RELEASE.md`).

Both hod binaries were used as they were built: release profile, neither

inputs), valgrind's callgrind for instruction counts, GNU `time` for peak
memory. Measured 2026-11-17 between 14:10 and 14:52 UTC.

## Method and its limits

- **One loaded host.** The shared development machine (aarch64, 24
  cores), with other release lanes and agents running. The load average
  (`uptime`) is beside every wall-time number: 21 to 30 throughout,
  against 13 to 26 for WP-288. Wall times and their ratios move with the
  load (below: the same table at load 16 to 24 and at 25 to 30); the
  **instruction counts are the firm numbers** (WP-292 measured their
  repeatability at 0.018% at worst; here the candidate's sums from the
  bench and from table 3's script agree to 0.01%).
- **x86-64 objects are a cross run** on this host (`--target x86_64`):
  hod and `llc` produce x86-64 objects on an aarch64 machine. Nothing was
  measured on a native x86-64 host.
- **Not measured against `clang -O0` in process.** `llc` is a separate
  process reading text IR, as hod is; clang's own back end run inside
  the compiler, without printing and reading the IR, was not measured.
- **hod on one thread.** In table 1's first pass hod inherited the shared
  jobserver (`MAKEFLAGS` exported by `scripts/env.sh`), which `cargo xtask
  bench` does not remove from hod's timed runs (only from the counted
  ones), so hod could take idle tokens on large modules. That pass is
  kept as a side record; table 1 below is the second pass, run with
  `MAKEFLAGS` unset and `HOD_THREADS=1`. WP-288's run sourced the same
  `env.sh`, so its wall times may have had that jobserver too; its
  instruction counts did not (counted runs are `HOD_THREADS=1` without
  `MAKEFLAGS`).
- End to end: aarch64 only, as in WP-288 (kiln cross-compiling to
  x86-64 needs a target sysroot the host does not have set up).
- Raw logs, results files and scripts are in the run directory
  `.build/release-1.1.0/perf/` (`run-bench.sh` with `bench-*.log` and
  `build/bench/results-*.tsv`; `pass1-jobserver/`; `e2e.sh`, `e2e/`,
  `e2e-sum.py`; `old-new.sh`, `old-new.tsv`, `old-new.log`,
  `old-new-summary.tsv`; `instr-llvm22.tsv`).

sources, `KILN_BACKEND=llvm` (kiln's LLVM path: `opt` where kiln runs it,
then `llc`) against `KILN_BACKEND=hod KILN_HOD=hod-a68a0d5d`, 5
interleaved rounds, `MAKEFLAGS` unset (hod on one thread). aarch64. Load
23.46 at the start, 21.09 at the end (14:46 to 14:48 UTC). Whole job:
fastest of 5 wall times by the script's clock. Back end: kiln's
`KILN_STATS_LOG` child-tool time (`children_ms`, summed) of the round with

Exact instruction sums: aarch64 5593804126 (1.0.0) and 4491008460
(candidate); x86-64 5710772286 and 4605659400. The candidate executes
19.7% (aarch64) and 19.4% (x86-64) fewer instructions over the 20
inputs: 26% to 30% fewer on stl-heavy-g, containers-g, tables, tables-g
and tiny-functions-g, 24% on templates-g, 4% to 9% on the inputs
without `-g` other than `tables` and `tiny-functions`. The 1.0.0 sums
are within 0.1% of WP-288's (5594 M and 5714 M); per input the largest
difference is 2.0 M (x86-64 templates-g, 913.4 against 915.4 M), the
inputs having been produced again by the same clang in another build
directory.

**Wall time did not follow the instructions here.** The summed fastest
times are equal (0.98x and 1.00x) at load 22 to 26, and the per-input
ratios scatter from 0.74 to 1.18 in both directions, on small inputs too
where the instruction counts barely differ. At this load a 20% cut in
user-mode instructions of a 20 to 200 ms one-thread process is inside
the run-to-run spread (the bench's own `llc` spread, middle over fastest,
was a median 7.8% and up to 22.7% in table 1's aarch64 run), and part of
each time is process start and page faults of the binary from the
bind-mounted workspace (WP-288 section 3), which the instruction cuts do
not touch. This run can neither confirm nor rule out a wall-time gain;
a quiet host would be needed for that. Peak memory moved by at most
3.6 MiB, down on the large inputs (stl-heavy-g 36.1 to 32.5 MiB), up by
up to 0.6 MiB on some small ones.
