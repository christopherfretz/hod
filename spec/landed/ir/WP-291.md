# WP-291 — symbols and constants in `hod_ir`, cheaper

Landed: `Hasher64::finish` (`crates/hod_ir/src/intern.rs`) folds the
state's high half into its low half and multiplies once more before it
takes the 32-bit hash; `Symbol::kind` (`crates/hod_ir/src/symbol.rs`)
is `#[inline]`. Tests: `hash_low_bits_see_the_top_of_the_last_word`
(`intern.rs`), `ids_follow_first_mention_across_growth` (`symbol.rs`),
`ids_follow_first_interning_across_growth` (`constant.rs`). No spec
text changes: `spec/ir.md` does not describe the hash function.

## What was wrong

WP-288 ranked `SymbolTable::named` (12.1 M), `ConstPool::intern`
(10.1 M) and `Symbol::kind` as 22 M of the 99 M instructions of the
`tables` bench input. Callgrind's line counts put almost all of the
first two in `InternTable`'s probe loops (`find_here` and `place`, about
16 M): long linear-probe chains, not hashing, key copies or comparisons.

The cause was the hash. `Hasher64` is FxHash-style: each word is
`state = (rotl(state, 5) ^ w) * K`. Bit `i` of a product depends only
on bits `0..=i` of the multiplicand, and `finish` returned
`state >> 32`, so the low bits of the hash, which `InternTable` indexes
with (`hash & mask`), depend only on bits `0..32+k` of the last word.
Keys that differ only in the top bytes of their last word collided in
their slot bits:

- the names `.str.100` to `.str.999` (8 bytes, one word; the digits are
  bytes 5 to 7): 900 names took 4 distinct values of the low 10 bits;
- `double` constants (`n / 1024.0 + 0.5`; the low mantissa bits are
  zero): 1000 constants took 1 value of the low 10 bits.

`finish` is now `((s ^ (s >> 32)) * K) >> 32`: after the fold the low
half carries the top bits, and the second product's bits 32 and up
depend on every bit of the fold. Same examples: 606 and 630 distinct
values of 1024 (a uniform hash gives about 600). Three more
instructions a hash.

`Symbol::kind` was not inlined into its callers (about 29 calls per
function: 145,000 on tiny-functions-g, 46 instructions each, building
the whole `SymbolKind::Function` with its attribute slices); most
callers read one field. Inlined, the unread fields are not built.

## Order

The hash decides only which slot holds an id. Ids are assigned in
insertion order and every table iterates its arena, never its slots, so
symbol, constant, string, type, attribute and debug-node ids, and every
order that reaches the object file, are unchanged. The two `ids_follow`
tests pin that across thousands of insertions and table growths.

## What was tried and did not pay, or was left

- The probe chains after the fix average about four slots at the 3/4
  load factor; a 1/2 load factor would save well under 1 M on tables and
  double the slot arrays, so it was not tried against the memory rule.
- What remains of the named functions on tables (`ConstPool::intern`
  about 2.9 M with `inline_hash`, `InternTable::grow` 0.8 M,
  `ConstPool::get` 2.2 M, a reader) is per-constant work of about 100
  instructions; no further cut was worth a change in shape.
