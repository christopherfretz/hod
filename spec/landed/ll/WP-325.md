# WP-325: the cost of reading text IR, the lexer (measured; no change)

Base `73165b3c`. Outcome: no source file changed. The lexer's inner
loops already run at about four instructions per byte, keywords are
already found by a perfect hash, `parse_type` already decides the
common types from the token, and the lexer copies nothing. The one
candidate cut left inside the lexer (an eight-bytes-at-once class test)
was built and measured: it costs 3.1% to 5.3% more on every input, and
was dropped. The headroom that is left is tokens lexed twice, and its
causes are outside the lexer (below, "What remains").

## Method

As WP-322: callgrind instruction counts of the whole program, release
profile, `HOD_THREADS=1`, `hod --strict -O0 -filetype=obj
-relocation-model=pic [-mtriple=x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu] -o out.o
in.ll`, on the bench inputs (`cargo xtask bench --generate`, and
`--target x86_64`), on the AArch64 build host. Functions are ranked by
their own (exclusive) cost only. A variant is a release build of the
base with one change, judged by its whole-program count on both
targets. Token counts come from a scratch build with counters in
`Lexer::next_token` and in the two replay paths (not committed), and
from lexing each input once from its first byte to its end with a fresh
`Lexer` (a scratch test, not committed).

## Step 0: is there headroom

"Lexer own" is the sum of the exclusive cost of every
`hod_ll::lexer::Lexer::*` function on x86-64 (with what is inlined into
them, such as the keyword lookup); lexing inlined into callers
(`parse_type`, `Parser::bump`, the label scan) is not in it. WP-322's
48 M counted only the `lexer.rs` lines of `next_token` and `word`.
AArch64 differs by at most 1.5 M per input.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| stl-heavy | 5,904,440 | 825,106 | 1,121,064 | 224,234 | 896,830 | 71,724 (8.7%) | 92.2 | 15.6 | 103 |
| stl-heavy-g | 16,290,921 | 2,819,008 | 3,305,593 | 319,596 | 2,985,997 | 166,989 (5.9%) | 226.0 | 13.9 | 76 |
| templates | 4,579,713 | 818,419 | 1,005,968 | 134,527 | 871,441 | 53,022 (6.5%) | 78.7 | 17.2 | 90 |
| tiny-functions | 1,287,160 | 260,441 | 310,647 | 198 | 310,449 | 50,008 (19.2%) | 24.3 | 18.9 | 78 |
| huge-function | 3,393,548 | 684,621 | 1,222,694 | 65,529 | 1,157,165 | 472,544 (69.0%) | 102.6 | 30.2 | 89 |

"Lexed" is calls minus replays; "lexed more than once" is lexed minus
the tokens of the text.

Token kinds (in the text). `stl-heavy`: words 312 k (38%), `%N` 115 k,
commas 106 k, integers 93 k, `=` 44 k, `iN` 34 k, parentheses 26 k
each, `@name` 13 k (92 bytes on average: mangled names), `%"name"` 10 k,
metadata names and `!` 6 to 7 k each. `stl-heavy-g`: integers 534 k,
commas 457 k, words 374 k, labels 334 k (the `line:` field names of
debug nodes), `!` 331 k, metadata names 135 k, `%N` 124 k. 89% of the
word and `iN` tokens lexed on `stl-heavy` are keywords.

How the lexer works today (`crates/hod_ll/src/lexer.rs`): a 256-entry
dispatch table on the first byte; a 256-entry class table for every
run, eight bytes per iteration; words of at most 15 bytes with 16 bytes
of source after them take one fast path that makes the keyword key from
two loads; keywords are a perfect hash (`kw.rs`: the word zero-padded
to 16 bytes as two words, a multiply-shift into 4,096 slots, one
compare: cut 1 is in place). A token is a kind, a keyword code and a
span: nothing is copied (cut 5 has nothing to take). `parse_type` maps
`iN` and the word types straight from the keyword code (cut 4 is in
place). Own costs on `stl-heavy` x86-64 (M): `word` 33.3, `next_token`
21.5, `global` 8.1, `at_end` (replay) 6.5, `local` 6.1, `after_blank`
6.0, `number` 3.4, `number_rule` 2.5, `compound` 2.4. A run of class
bytes costs about four instructions per byte (two loads, a test, a
branch); the rest is per token, about 50 to 80 instructions.

Where tokens are lexed twice:

- `huge-function`: the first forward branch in the 90,000-line body
  makes `Fx::scan_labels` (`body.rs`) lex the rest of the body to find
  every label. It records at most `Recording::MAX` (65,536) tokens for
  the parse to replay; past that the parse lexes them again: about
  470 k tokens, some 40 M instructions (11% of the program). The cap is
  memory: 16 bytes per token, about 7.5 MB more for this body, against
  a peak of about 11.8 MB (the counts gate fails at 2% and 512 KiB).
- every input: the index scan (`index.rs`, `define` and
  `rest_of_entity`) lexes each `define`'s header outside the parameter
  list and every `declare`, and the parser lexes them again: about ten
  tokens per function (50 k on `tiny-functions`, 72 k on `stl-heavy`).
- `skip_group` lexes nothing: it steps over bytes, a word at a time
  (five byte matches per word), at 10 to 17 M own on `stl-heavy`.
- a peek re-lexes only in the index scan's `skim_type`, after a
  `prefix`, `prologue` or `personality`.

## Cut tried and dropped: the class test eight bytes at once (cut 2)

`skip_while` for the classes `DIGIT`, `WORD` and `LABEL`, and the run
length in `word`, by arithmetic on eight bytes at once (a range test per
byte range, no table). Exact (a test compared it with the table for
every byte in every position), and slower: tokens are short (six bytes
on average), and the constants of the arithmetic are made again at
every call (on AArch64 a 64-bit constant is four instructions);
`skip_while` was also no longer inlined. `word` went from 33.3 M to
45.0 M own on `stl-heavy`.

built: counted per eight bytes it is about 30 instructions in a loop,
against about 32 for the table loop, so no gain is to be had.

Cuts 1, 4 and 5 have nothing to take (above). Cut 3 is the remaining
headroom, and is not in the lexer's files.

## What remains

- Tokens past `Recording::MAX` lexed twice (`huge-function`, up to
  about 40 M). A recording that keeps more tokens in the same memory
  would take part of it: about four bytes per token (kind, keyword
  code, gap and length in a byte each, the offset looked for at taken
  from the previous token's end, with a full entry where a token does
  not fit or the scan jumped over a parenthesis group) holds 262 k
  tokens in today's 1 MiB. `Lexer::seek` finds an entry by offset
  (`partition_point`), which such a recording does not allow without a
  side index.
- Headers lexed by the index scan and again by the parser (about ten
  tokens per function; about 4 M on `stl-heavy`, 3 M on
  `tiny-functions` net of the replay). It needs the index to keep the
  tokens and the module parse to replay them (`index.rs`, `globals.rs`),
  and memory for them (16 bytes per token today).
- Own costs on `stl-heavy` x86-64 (M): lexer 92.2 (above), `skip_group`
  16.7, `parse_type` 12.9.
