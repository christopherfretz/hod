# WP-173 — x86 target intrinsics, SSE to SSSE3

Landed: `crates/hod_x64/src/x86.rs` (new; its hook at the start of
`intrinsic::intrinsic`, its row in `lib.rs`'s module table), `hod_ir`'s
98 `Intrinsic` members `X86Sse*`/`X86Sse2*`/`X86Sse3*`/`X86Ssse3*`
(`crates/hod_ir/src/intrinsic.rs`, with `cmp.ss`/`cmp.sd`'s predicate as
an immediate argument) and their matrix rows `INTRINSIC_X86`
(`never(X64_ONLY)`, `on(X86_SSE)`; `crates/hod_ir/src/classify/matrix.rs`),
their signatures in `hod_ir/tests/body_llvm.rs`, `docs/status/support.md`,
`spec/codegen.md` §5.6.22. Tests: `crates/hod_x64/tests/x86_intrinsics.rs`.

## What is built and what is refused

- Built: the 98 rows above marked "built", SSE and SSE2 in every x86-64
  function, SSE3 and SSSE3 where the function's `target-features` enable
  them. hod writes SSE2 instructions only (§5.6.2); SSE3 and SSSE3 are
  computed with them and with general-register lane loops
  (`x86_intrinsics_agree_with_llc` disassembles hod's code and finds no
  later instruction). `pmovmskb.128` builds: the corpus module
  `conformance/target.x86/groupcontrol-sse2-run.cpp` (kiln, LLVM 22 and
  19 IR), refused by its name before, compiles with `hod --strict` and
  under `qemu-x86_64` prints what `llc`'s build prints.
- Refused by name, "no encoding": `hod_mc_x64` has no `ldmxcsr`,
  `stmxcsr`, `rcpps`/`rcpss`, `rsqrtps`/`rsqrtss`, `clflush`,
  `maskmovdqu`, `monitor`, `mwait`. These names are not on `hod_ir`'s
  list, so `classify` refuses them (`intrinsic:llvm.x86.sse.ldmxcsr`, ...).
  Approximations (`rcp`, `rsqrt`) could be computed exactly with
  `divps`/`sqrtps`, but their results would differ from the hardware's
  in the low bits; not done.
- Refused by name, not built yet: every `sse41.*` and `sse42.*` intrinsic
  and the MMX forms.
- Refused by name in a function without the feature: the 19 SSE3 and
  SSSE3 names (`intrinsic:llvm.x86.sse3.hadd.ps`, ...).
- AArch64 refuses every `llvm.x86.*` name (`never(X64_ONLY)`).

## Evidence

Every leg runs under `qemu-x86_64`: hod plain (bfd and lld), hod under
every stress option (bfd) on the baseline CPU (`qemu64,-sse3,...`), and
`llc -O0 -fast-isel=0 -relocation-model=pic` 22 and 19 on that CPU with
`sse3` and `ssse3`; `llc` 22 is the reference.

| check | what | numbers |
|---|---|---|
| `x86_intrinsics_agree_with_llc` | each of the 98 intrinsics on 40 input sets (edge floats and doubles: NaNs with payloads, infinities, signed zeros, denormals, the conversions' bounds; edge integers; random; all-ones), shifts by the constant counts 0, 1, 3, 15, 16, 31, 32, 63, 64, 300 and by variable and vector counts, `cmp.ss`/`cmp.sd` with predicates 0 to 7, 9 and 31, each two-operand intrinsic also with one operand twice, `ldu.dq` from an odd address | 255 functions, 10,200 cases, 5 legs, no disagreement; text 19,396 bytes against `llc` 22's 4,988 (3.89×; mostly the SSSE3 lane loops) |
| `above_the_baseline_only_with_the_feature` | each SSE3/SSSE3 intrinsic without features, with `+sse,+sse2`, with its feature then `-sse3`, (SSSE3) with `+sse3` only: refused by its name; with its feature, `+sse4.2`, `+avx2`: built | 19 intrinsics, 6 modules each (7 for SSSE3) |
| `rows_and_refusals_by_name` | x86-64 supports every row, AArch64 none; `ldmxcsr` has no row and `classify` names it; a wrong signature is `x64:x86-intrinsic-signature` | 98 rows |
| corpus | `groupcontrol-sse2-run.cpp.ll`, LLVM 22 and 19 | was `intrinsic:llvm.x86.sse2.pmovmskb.128`; output equal to `llc`'s |

Mutations (each caught, then reverted):

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| `comi`/`ucomi` `lt` and `le` without swapping the operands | 597 disagreements |
| `pshufb` without zeroing the bytes whose selector has bit 7 set | 216 disagreements |
| the `i16` saturation's upper bound `0x7ffe` | 201 disagreements |

NaN results: `qemu-x86_64` gives what the hardware's rules give for the
instructions both builds use; where hod's instruction differs from
`llc`'s (SSE3 and SSSE3), the operations order their operands as the
instruction does (`hadd` adds lane `2i` to `2i+1`, `hsub` subtracts the
odd lane from the even), so the NaN returned is the same operand.

## Cost on the bench inputs

The bench inputs (clang 22, x86-64) call no `llvm.x86.*` intrinsic: 1,234
intrinsic calls over the ten sources, none x86. Each now passes one
`match` on the `Intrinsic` member (`x86::row`, `None`) before the
existing dispatch: some tens of instructions per call, about 10⁴ in
all, against counts of 10⁷ to 10⁹ per input. Not measured with
callgrind (the shared host was loaded and the change cannot show at
that size).
