# WP-096 — hod_ll: throughput of the text route

Landed: four cuts in `crates/hod_ll` (each its own commit, below; a
fifth was measured, found wanting and withdrawn), `spec/ll.md` §11.1
(who owns the storage a body is built in), the tools
`scripts/phase-profile.py`, `scripts/gdb-sample.py`,
`scripts/corpus-bytes.py` and `crates/hod_ll/examples/profile.rs`, and the
deterministic checks in `crates/hod_ll/tests/throughput.rs` and the unit
tests named per cut.

Target not met: body reading is 15 to 22% cheaper per instruction on the
large units (2 to 7% on the pathological ones), not half (see "What is
left"). The module parse is 4 to 16% cheaper as a side effect of the
lexer cuts.

## How it was measured

The machine (aarch64 container, load 18 to 26 throughout) has no `perf`,
`strace`, `valgrind` or hardware counters. What works:

- `scripts/phase-profile.py`: the release `hod` binaries alternated run by
  run, `HOD_THREADS=1`, input on a pipe as kiln feeds it, the
  `HOD_TIME_PHASES` table (median / minimum of 9 or 11 runs, µs), and the
  process' deterministic counters: read/write system calls and bytes
  (`/proc/<pid>/io` read while the child is a zombie), minor and major
  faults and peak RSS (`wait4`).
- `crates/hod_ll/examples/profile.rs`: the module parse and body reading
  repeated in one process, so the binary's pages are faulted in once
  (the workspace's bind mount has no page cache: every exec takes about
  20 major faults, which on a small input is most of `parse`), with ns per
  instruction; `lex` mode lexes the whole file.
- `scripts/gdb-sample.py`: gdb attached a few hundred times to the
  `profile` example in a long loop; self, inclusive, and self charged to
  the first frame in `crates/` (library `memcmp`, `malloc`, iterator
  helpers charged to their caller's line).

"base" is the branch point `c089c09`; "head" is `1df0390`.

Where the time goes inside the phases that are `hod_ll`'s:

- **read** is `read_to_end` on the pipe in 64 KiB reads (73 system calls
  for 3.2 MB) and the faults of the growing buffer; the writer is the
  bottleneck, not hod. From a file on standard input it is the same
  number of calls. Not cut.
- **write** is one `write` (3 calls in all: object, two for the phase
  file); to a pipe 4 to 12 µs of work, to a file on the bind mount 5.6 ms
  of the file system. Not cut.
- **parse** (`-O0`, in process): the index scan is a third (regex: scan
  1.4 of 4.1 ms), the rest the declarations and attribute groups. With
  `-g` it is the largest phase: 60% building debug nodes (`node_body`
  34%: each field looked up by name in `hod_ir`'s tables three times,
  `di_field` alone 5.5%, a 48-slice array cleared per node, byte
  comparisons 7%), 24% the index scan of the metadata lines (lexing
  them), 10% `hod_ir`'s `DebugInfo::store` and `DiNode::encode`.
- **bodies** (in process, base): 413 to 441 ns per instruction on the
  regex unit, 418 to 434 on stl-heavy, 325 to 346 on templates, 247 to 252
  on huge-function, 253 to 256 on tiny-functions, 533 to 543 on the regex
  unit with `-g` (hod_bc: 219). Of that, on the regex unit (204 samples):
  the lexer 35 to 40% self; the label scan (`scan_labels`, one forward
  pass over the rest of a body at its first forward branch) 18%
  inclusive; the attribute table's binary search with its `memcmp` 11%;
  growth of the body's tables by doubling 6.4%; calls 32% inclusive, of
  which `param_attrs` 13% and `parse_type` 12.7%. A body's text is 80
  bytes and 11.5 tokens per instruction on stl-heavy (a fifth of the
  bytes are mangled names), and the lexer costs 1.44 ns per byte, 10.2 per
  token: the lexer is about a third of body reading and is paid about
  1.7 times per token in a body with branches (the walk and the label
  scan).

Deterministic counters, base and head alike on every input: read and write
calls and bytes are identical (regex `-O0`: 73 reads, 3 writes).

## The cuts

ns per instruction, body reading in process, minimum of 11 (three
interleaved rounds each):

| cut | commit | what | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `0957001`, amended in `1df0390` | Bodies are built in the parser's own storage and `parse` returns an exact-size copy (`spec/ll.md` §11.1); `parse_bodies` hands out the storage itself. The CLI never recycles, so every body grew its tables by doubling from empty and the session kept the slack. The amendment: a body of `HAND_OVER_BYTES` (256 KiB of tables) or more is the storage itself, handed over, since a copy held it twice (huge-function: peak RSS +1.1 MiB, +587 minor faults before the amendment, the branch point's after). | stl-heavy 427–500 → 395–416 |
| 2 | `d69fbb8` | A direct-mapped memo (64 slots, key: the word packed in 16 bytes) in front of `attr_info` for parameter and function attribute keywords. | stl-heavy → 355–363 |
| 3 | `87c9b43` | The lexer skips white space and metadata names by class (two new class bits) with one slice scan per run. | lexing 10.2 → 9.1–9.5 ns/token; stl-heavy 371 → 359, regex 370–377 → 355–372 |
| 4 | `ff1efae` | The lexer's fast paths: a class bit for what can start a blank or a comment, so the next token is found after one or two byte tests; no label run asked for after a word or number that ends at a byte that can neither continue nor end a label. | lexing → 8.8 ns/token; stl-heavy 355 → 344–350, regex 343–350 → 334–338 |
| (5) | `06b234b`, withdrawn in `746266c` | Debug nodes: a memo per (kind, field name) for `di_field`, `field_shape` and the `'static` name; the fields met kept in reused scratch as packed names. | regex `-g` module parse 2 to 3% faster (noise), huge-function `-g` 5 to 8% slower, with a direct-mapped table and with per-kind lists: withdrawn |

Their checks (counts and answers, never time):
`bodies_are_handed_over_without_spare_capacity` and
`parse_bodies_and_parse_agree` (cut 1),
`a_large_body_is_handed_over_not_copied` (cut 1's amendment),
`attribute_keywords_are_looked_up_once` in `body.rs` (cut 2: 20 functions
of 40 calls search the table 5 to 8 times),
`classes_are_the_sets_of_section_2` in `lexer.rs` (cuts 3 and 4: every
class bit for all 256 bytes). `field_rules_of_debug_nodes` stays from the
withdrawn cut: repeated, missing, long and nested fields of debug nodes
refused with the same text at the same offset, which no test covered
(checked against the base binary on the command line too).

Tried, no gain, reverted: caching the tokens the label scan lexes so that
the walk reads them instead of lexing again, both as the scan's own
buffer (+4%) and as a look-ahead of the whole body served to `bump` (5 to
10% slower: storing and matching 12-byte tokens costs what lexing them
again does). A plain index loop for `skip_while`, and `#[inline]` on the
lexer's `compound`: no change.

## hod_bc

- Cut 1 applies: `crates/hod_bc/src/body.rs:226` hands out the body built
  in storage that grew by doubling (`Ok(body) => Ok(Some(body))`), and
  `recycle` (`body.rs:166`) is the only way back; the CLI does not
  recycle there either.
- Cut 2 hardly applies: `crates/hod_bc/src/attrs.rs:284` and `:352` search
  `attr_info` per attribute of a group, once per group, not per call.
- Cuts 3 and 4 are the text lexer's.

## What is left (the next three cuts)

1. The label scan, 10 to 15% of body reading: a body with a forward
   branch is lexed about 1.7 times. Resolving forward block references
   lazily (a block's ordinal fixed at its label, the references patched
   then) would remove the scan, but needs an entry point added to
   `hod_ir`'s `FunctionBuilder` to retarget a block operand, and must
   fall back to the present scan on any failure so that the first
   refusal stays the same (a later label with a value's name is refused
   at the value with the scan, at the label without it).
2. The lexer's per-token cost (8.8 ns, about 3 mispredicted run ends per
   token): one dispatch table for the first byte that also classifies
   the token's run, and word-at-a-time scanning of long name runs (a
   fifth of a body's bytes are mangled names of 16 bytes or more).
3. Calls (30% of body reading on stl-heavy): `param_attrs` per argument
   (`noundef nonnull align 8 dereferenceable(16)` re-read and re-built
   for every call) could be memoized by the attribute text's span, and
   the per-body `Parser` construction (a large struct moved in and out,
   1 to 2% on tiny functions) kept across bodies.
