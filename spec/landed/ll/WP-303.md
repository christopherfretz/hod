# WP-303: module-level work on several threads (step 1: the index scan)

Item 4 of the ranked list of `spec/landed/harness/WP-288.md`. This is the
first of up to three sessions; what it built is the index scan of
`spec/ll.md` §4.1 on the threads hod is granted (`spec/ll.md` §4.4).

## Measured first (base `eb7ae0d2`)

Fifteen interleaved runs per configuration on the shared host (load
about 12 to 14 of 24 cores), phase report of `hod -O0 -filetype=obj
-relocation-model=pic`, minimum/median in ms. At 8 threads on
`stl-heavy-g` (16 MB of `-g` text, either target): `read` 7 to 9 / 9,
**`parse` 46 / 48**, `bodies` 9.5 / 10, `emission` 13 / 13.5, `object`
10.5 / 11, `write` 3.5 / 5, total 94 / 99. The module-level `parse` is
about half of the wall time at 8 threads, so item 4 is still worth it;
`object` and `write` together are 14 to 16 ms, `read` is a file read.

The module-level parse of `stl-heavy-g`, timed inside `parser::run`
(`hod_ll` example `profile`, one thread): index scan 11 to 16 ms, the
debug nodes (`build_debug`) 28 ms (the node loop 26, `unique_nodes` 3),
global values 2.3, everything else under 2.5. Callgrind of the same, of
470 M instructions: the scan 115 M (of which `index`/`skip` 73 M, lexer
38 M); outside it the lexer and keyword lookup 117 M, `hod_ll::debug`
146 M, `hod_ir`'s debug tables, interning and strings about 40 M, the
rest about 50 M.

What is independent work: the scan (a function of the source alone,
cut at entity starts); the lexing inside the debug nodes (a token is a
function of the source and an offset, `spec/ll.md` §2.8). Not
independent: everything that hands out ids or interns (`reserve`,
`DebugInfo::add`, constants, `Made::define`), which `build_debug` does
interleaved with the reading, in node order.

## Built

`hod_ll::parse_module_on(source, options, threads)` and
`scan_threads(len, granted)` (`spec/ll.md` §4.4): pieces of about
`SCAN_PIECE_BYTES` (256 KiB) cut at line starts that begin with `!`, `@`
or `define `, taken in turn by `scan_threads` threads (one per
`SCAN_BYTES_PER_THREAD`, 1 MiB, at most `SCAN_MAX_THREADS`, 8), joined in
source order; a piece that did not begin at the token the previous one
ended at is scanned again on the calling thread. One thread runs the
serial scan. `hod`'s text reader asks for the scan's threads through the
compile's one request to the pool (`crates/hod/src/front/read.rs`,
`scanning_threads`), so the tokens are held for reading and code
generation as before.

Pieces taken in turn rather than one fixed range per thread: with equal
byte ranges two threads gave 8.6 ms of 11.6 (the halves cost unequally:
bodies are stepped over at 5 GiB/s, metadata is dense small entities);
taken in turn, 6.4.

The serial loop and the pieces share one out-of-line body
(`Scanner::run`): with two instantiations (a const generic) LLVM stopped
inlining `Scanner::node` and `expect` into the serial scan, +0.36%
instructions at one thread on `stl-heavy-g`; with one body the count is
the base's (+0.001%).

Thresholds, measured with the `profile` example (`module-on:<n>`) on
`stl-heavy-g`: the scan takes 11.6 ms on one thread, 6.4 on two, 3.7 on
four, 2.2 on eight; a thread costs 20 to 60 µs to start and join
(`spec/landed/cli/WP-043.md`) against about 0.7 ms per MiB of scan, so one
thread per MiB pays with a wide margin and keeps small modules (all of
the corpus but a few) serial.

## Ruled out, and why

- Fixed ranges, one per thread: unbalanced (above).
- Splitting `build_debug` itself (ids and interning order): every node
  read interleaves module mutations (`reserve`, string and node
  interning, constants, tuples defined last-in first-out) with the
  lexing; splitting it needs the reading split into a pure pre-parse
  and an ordered replay, a rewrite of `debug.rs` whose one-thread cost
  would be hard to keep within ±0.1%. Not this session.
- `object`/`write` overlap: 14 to 16 ms together at 8 threads, below the
  remaining parse cost; ranked after the debug-node lexing.

## What remains (in order)

1. Lexing of the debug nodes ahead of `build_debug` on the granted
   threads, replayed through the lexer's existing replay (`spec/ll.md`
   §2.8): about 117 M instructions of lexing outside the scan on
   `stl-heavy-g`, a token being a function of the source and its offset
   alone, so byte identity holds by construction; the serial path
   unchanged when no recording is given. Open: memory of the recordings
   (16 bytes a token, about 2 M tokens) means pieces handed over in
   order and freed as replayed, not one recording.
2. The `object` phase split or overlapped (layout and `to_vec`).
3. The index join copies every piece's entries (a few ms of memcpy at
   most); appending in place or sizing first if it shows in a profile.
